For mostly everyday photos, I’d pick the **iPhone 17 Pro**: its colour, portraits and results across lenses were more consistent in a direct camera comparison. Pick the **Pixel 10 Pro** if distant subjects, fine detail and Google’s editing tools matter more.[^1]

| What you shoot | Better fit |
| --- | --- |
| **Everyday scenes** | **iPhone.** More dependable colour and dynamic range in Tech Advisor’s tests; the Pixel often captured finer detail but sometimes shifted colours.[^1] |
| **People and selfies** | **iPhone.** The same comparison favoured it for portraits and selfies.[^1] |
| **Travel and distant subjects** | **Pixel.** Its 5× telephoto and stronger long-range zoom give it more reach. At 30× and beyond, though, its Pro Res Zoom uses generative AI, so added detail isn’t necessarily faithful to the scene.[^1] |
| **Low light** | **Depends on the scene.** The Pixel captured sharp detail and offers astrophotography mode; the iPhone showed more accurate white balance and better overall scene detail in the comparison’s low-light example.[^1] |
| **Close-ups** | **Both are capable.** The Pixel showed better central detail in one macro test; the iPhone produced more natural-looking edges.[^1] |

Sam, since you also make videos and Reels, the iPhone is the safer all-round choice: the same review found its video cleaner. But if you’re primarily drawn to travel shots with distant details—and prefer Android—the Pixel is a compelling alternative. Compare sample photos before buying: the difference in processing style may matter more to you than the specifications.[^1]
